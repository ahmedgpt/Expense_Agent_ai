# Budgeting Methods: 50/30/20, Zero-Based and Envelope

## 50/30/20 rule
Split take-home income into three buckets: 50% needs (rent, utilities, groceries, transport, minimum debt payments), 30% wants (dining out, entertainment, shopping, hobbies), 20% savings and extra debt repayment. It is a starting point; in high-rent cities the needs share may reach 60% and wants must shrink.

## Zero-based budgeting
Every rupee of income is assigned a job so that income minus planned spending, saving and debt payments equals zero. Useful for irregular incomes: re-plan at the start of each month and adjust categories when income changes.

## Envelope method
Each category gets a fixed amount (physically or virtually). When an envelope is empty, spending in that category stops or is covered by explicitly moving money from another envelope. Works well for volatile categories such as dining out and shopping.

## Choosing a method
- Beginners with stable salary: 50/30/20.
- Freelancers or variable income: zero-based.
- Chronic overspending in one category: envelope.

## Agent guidance
When a user asks for a budget, ask for monthly take-home income, fixed obligations and goals, then propose a method and show the arithmetic. Always store the user's accepted budget limits in long-term memory.
